The Treatment

What Is Spinal Decompression?

Spinal decompression is a computer-controlled stretch of the spine. You lie comfortably on a padded table while it applies a slow, precise pull to the lower back or neck.

The stretch creates space between the vertebrae. That lowers pressure inside the disc, takes load off irritated nerves, and helps water, oxygen, and nutrients move back into the disc so it can heal.

It is not the same as an inversion table or basic traction. The table cycles between stretch and relaxation, so your muscles stay relaxed instead of guarding against the pull.

How It Works

What Decompression Does for Your Spine

Discs don't have much blood supply of their own. Decompression changes the pressure inside the disc so it can take in what it needs to repair.

01

Creates Space

A slow, controlled stretch gently separates the vertebrae and opens the space around the disc and nerve roots.

02

Lowers Disc Pressure

The stretch creates negative pressure inside the disc, which can help draw bulging or herniated material back toward the center.

03

Takes Pressure Off Nerves

Less pressure on the nerve root can mean less pain, numbness, and tingling down the leg or arm.

04

Brings in Nutrients

The pumping effect of stretch and release helps water, oxygen, and nutrients move into the disc to support healing.

05

Relaxes Tight Muscles

Cycled stretch and release lets muscles around the spine relax instead of tightening up and fighting the treatment.

06

Supports Long-Term Results

Paired with adjustments and home exercises, decompression helps you build a stronger, more stable spine over time.

Is It Right for You?

Who Is a Good Candidate?

Decompression works best for the right patient. Your exam tells us if you're one of them.

You may be a good fit if you:

  • Have back or neck pain lasting more than a few weeks
  • Have pain, numbness, or tingling going down a leg or arm
  • Have been told you have a bulging or herniated disc
  • Tried pills, injections, or PT without lasting relief
  • Want to avoid or delay spine surgery

Decompression may not be right if you:

  • Are pregnant
  • Have had a spinal fusion or have hardware in the spine
  • Have severe osteoporosis or a recent spinal fracture
  • Have a spinal tumor or infection
  • Have loss of bladder or bowel control (call your doctor right away)

Not sure? That's what the exam is for. If decompression isn't the right tool, we'll point you to what is.

Common Questions

Spinal Decompression FAQ

Does spinal decompression hurt?
No. Most patients feel a gentle stretch in the low back or neck. Many find it relaxing. If anything feels uncomfortable, we adjust the settings right away.
How many sessions will I need?
It depends on your condition and how long you've had it. Most plans involve a series of visits over several weeks. We give you a specific recommendation after your exam.
How is decompression different from traction or an inversion table?
Basic traction and inversion pull the spine with a steady force, which often makes muscles tighten up. Decompression is computer-controlled and cycles between stretch and relaxation, so muscles stay relaxed and the stretch reaches the disc.
Can spinal decompression help sciatica?
Sciatica is often caused by a disc pressing on a nerve in the low back. When that's the cause, decompression can help take pressure off the nerve. Your exam tells us whether a disc is the source of your sciatica.
Do you treat the neck as well as the low back?
Yes. Decompression can be set up for the lumbar spine (low back) or the cervical spine (neck), depending on where your problem is.
Is there any downtime after a session?
No. You can go back to work and normal activity right after. We may ask you to avoid heavy lifting early in your plan and give you simple exercises to do at home.
Does insurance cover spinal decompression?
Coverage varies by carrier and plan, and many plans do not cover decompression. Call us at (816) 895-1800 and we'll walk you through your options and costs before you start.
