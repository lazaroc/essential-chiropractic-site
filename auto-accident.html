First Steps

What to Do After a Car Accident

If you have just been in a car accident, here are the steps to take to protect both your health and your ability to recover what you are owed.

1
Get emergency medical care first

If you have signs of serious injury such as head trauma, broken bones, severe bleeding, loss of consciousness, or severe chest or abdominal pain, call 911 or go directly to the emergency room.

2
Document everything

Take photos of the scene, vehicle damage, license plates, and insurance cards. Collect contact information from witnesses.

3
File a police report

Even for minor accidents, an official report protects you later in case symptoms develop or claims become disputed.

4
Notify your insurance company

Most policies require prompt notification after an accident.

5
See a chiropractor or doctor within 72 hours

Many car accident injuries do not produce symptoms for 24 to 72 hours after the collision. The earlier you are evaluated, the easier it is to start care that supports recovery — and the easier it is to connect your symptoms to the accident for insurance purposes.

6
Keep a symptom journal

Note when symptoms start, what they feel like, and how they change over time. This information helps your care team and any insurance claim.

Important: If you have just been in an accident and have any signs of serious injury, seek emergency medical evaluation first. Chiropractic care supports recovery but is not a substitute for emergency or specialty medical care.

Common Auto Injuries

Common Auto Accident Injuries We Help With

Car accidents put stress on the spine, soft tissues, and nervous system in ways that are different from everyday wear and tear. Here are the most common injuries we see in our Lee's Summit office.

Neck Pain & Cervical Strain

Rear-end and side-impact collisions commonly produce neck pain, stiffness, and reduced range of motion. These injuries often involve the muscles, ligaments, and joints of the cervical spine.

Low Back Pain & Lumbar Strain

The seated position during a collision puts heavy strain on the low back. Many accident patients develop low-back pain in the hours and days following the impact, often combined with hip or sciatic-nerve symptoms.

Upper Back & Mid-Back Pain

The mid-back takes a heavy load during a collision, particularly from the seatbelt. Tension and pain in the area between the shoulder blades is common after even minor accidents.

Headaches & Tension Symptoms

Headaches that start at the base of the skull and travel forward are common after motor vehicle accidents. If you have any signs of concussion — including memory issues, severe headache, vision changes, or nausea — please seek medical evaluation before chiropractic care.

Shoulder & Arm Injuries

The seatbelt, steering wheel, and bracing for impact all contribute to shoulder and arm strain. We often see tightness, limited range of motion, and tingling in the arms after accidents.

Hip & Knee Strain

Drivers commonly brace against the floorboards during impact. Passengers often hit the dashboard with their knees. Both can lead to hip and knee discomfort that shows up days or weeks later.

Soft Tissue Injuries

Sprains, strains, and inflammation in the muscles, ligaments, and tendons are present in nearly every accident, even minor ones. Chiropractic care combined with soft tissue work aims to support the healing of these tissues.

Delayed Symptoms

Symptoms that appear 24 to 72 hours after a collision — or later — are extremely common. See the note below for why this matters.

Why Delayed Symptoms Matter

This is one of the most important things to understand about car accidents. Adrenaline and shock during and after a collision can mask injury symptoms for hours or days. It is very common for neck pain, low-back pain, headaches, and stiffness to begin 24 to 72 hours after the accident, or sometimes even later. Delayed symptoms are real injuries, and they are best addressed early.

If you experience signs of concussion, severe pain, weakness, numbness, or loss of function, seek medical evaluation before chiropractic care.

Whiplash Treatment

Whiplash Treatment in Lee's Summit

Whiplash is the most common single injury from car accidents. Here is what to know.

What Is Whiplash?

Whiplash is a rapid back-and-forth motion of the neck, most often caused by rear-end collisions. The motion stretches and strains the muscles, ligaments, and joints of the cervical spine, even when the collision happens at low speed.

Whiplash Symptoms

Common whiplash symptoms include:

  • Neck pain and stiffness
  • Headaches, often starting at the base of the skull
  • Reduced range of motion in the neck
  • Tingling or numbness in the arms
  • Fatigue and difficulty concentrating
  • Dizziness

Why Symptoms Are Often Delayed

The adrenaline and shock that follow a collision can mask symptoms. Most whiplash patients begin feeling pain within 24 to 72 hours, and some not for several days. This is normal, and it is one of the main reasons we recommend evaluation soon after an accident even if you feel fine.

How Chiropractic Care Supports Recovery

Gentle adjustments aim to restore normal motion in the cervical spine, reduce muscle and ligament tension, and support nerve communication. We commonly combine adjustments with soft tissue work and at-home exercises that fit your specific symptoms and stage of recovery.

Missouri Coverage

Missouri Auto Accident Coverage & Chiropractic Care

Missouri is an at-fault state for auto accidents. That means the driver responsible for the accident, and their insurance, is generally responsible for damages and medical expenses. Here is how that affects your care.

PIP

Personal Injury Protection

PIP coverage is available in Missouri but not required. If you have PIP on your policy, it can cover medical expenses including chiropractic care regardless of who was at fault for the accident.

Med-Pay

Medical Payments

Med-Pay is optional coverage that can pay for medical care after an accident, including chiropractic care. It is often a simpler way to access care quickly without waiting for fault to be determined.

Liability

Bodily Injury Liability

If another driver caused your accident, their liability coverage may be responsible for your medical care. This typically involves coordination with a personal injury attorney.

Health Plan

Health Insurance

If you do not have auto-specific medical coverage, your standard health insurance may cover chiropractic care after an accident. Coverage details vary by plan.

We will help you understand which of these options applies to your situation during your first call. We are not attorneys and the information above is general background, not legal advice. For questions about your specific case, please consult a Missouri personal injury attorney.

Common Questions

Auto Accident Chiropractic Care: Common Questions

Should I see a chiropractor after a car accident?
Yes, in most cases. Even minor accidents put stress on the spine and soft tissues that can become chronic if not addressed. A chiropractic evaluation helps identify what was affected and what needs care.
How long after a car accident should I see a chiropractor?
We recommend within 72 hours when possible. Earlier evaluation makes recovery easier and helps document your injuries for insurance purposes. If you cannot get in that quickly, sooner is still better than later.
Why am I feeling pain days after my accident?
Delayed pain is very common after car accidents. Adrenaline and shock mask symptoms during and after the collision. Most patients begin feeling pain 24 to 72 hours after the accident, sometimes longer. These delayed symptoms are real injuries and they are best addressed promptly.
Should I see a chiropractor or a regular doctor after a car accident?
You may want to see both. If you have any signs of serious injury, seek emergency medical care first. For musculoskeletal injuries like neck pain, back pain, and whiplash, chiropractic care is often the most direct path to addressing the cause. We are happy to coordinate with your medical doctor.
What is whiplash and how do you treat it?
Whiplash is a strain of the muscles, ligaments, and joints of the neck from rapid back-and-forth motion, most often from rear-end collisions. We support whiplash recovery with gentle adjustments, soft tissue work, and at-home exercises tailored to your symptoms.
Does insurance cover chiropractic care after a car accident?
In many cases, yes. Auto insurance policies often include PIP or Med-Pay coverage that can apply to chiropractic care. Health insurance may also cover accident-related care. Call us at (816) 895-1800 and we will help you confirm your coverage.
Will my chiropractic bill be covered by the at-fault driver's insurance?
If another driver caused your accident, their bodily injury liability coverage may be responsible for your medical care. This typically involves coordination with a personal injury attorney. We are happy to work with your attorney to provide documentation supporting your claim.
Can a chiropractor help with delayed back or neck pain?
Yes. Delayed pain after a car accident is one of the most common reasons patients come in. We assess what was affected during the accident and build a care plan to support recovery.
Do I need a referral from my doctor to see a chiropractor after an accident?
In most cases, no. You can schedule directly with our office. Some insurance plans may require a referral, so we recommend checking your specific coverage.
What if I don't have auto insurance coverage for medical care?
Many patients without PIP or Med-Pay coverage use their health insurance, work with an attorney on a lien basis, or pay through other arrangements. Call us and we will help you find the right path forward.
Do you work with personal injury attorneys?
Yes. We coordinate with personal injury attorneys throughout Lee's Summit and the Kansas City area. We provide documentation, treatment notes, and billing records to support your claim.
How many chiropractic visits will I need after a car accident?
That depends on the severity of your injuries, how long it has been since the accident, and how your body responds to care. After your initial exam, we will discuss what we recommend and roughly how long care typically takes for an injury like yours.
