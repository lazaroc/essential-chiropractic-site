The Basics

What Is Prenatal Chiropractic Care?

Prenatal chiropractic care is the gentle assessment and adjustment of the spine, pelvis, and surrounding soft tissues during pregnancy, using techniques designed for the safety and comfort of the expecting mother and her baby.

During pregnancy, the body goes through significant changes. The growing belly shifts your center of gravity forward, your posture adapts, and the hormone relaxin softens ligaments throughout the body, particularly in the pelvis. These changes can create tension, discomfort, and joint instability.

Prenatal chiropractic care focuses on supporting pelvic alignment, easing tension in the low back and hips, and helping the body adapt to a healthy, comfortable pregnancy. The American Pregnancy Association recognizes chiropractic care as a complementary approach during pregnancy.

What pregnancy asks of your body

  • A growing belly shifts your center of gravity forward
  • Posture adapts, adding load to the low back and hips
  • The hormone relaxin softens pelvic ligaments

Our Specialty

The Webster Technique

The Webster Technique is the most specialized prenatal chiropractic approach we offer. It is recognized by chiropractors, doulas, midwives, and many obstetricians as a gentle, supportive method during pregnancy.

What Is the Webster Technique?

The Webster Technique is a specific chiropractic analysis and adjustment used during pregnancy. It focuses on the sacrum and the surrounding ligaments of the pelvis, with the goal of supporting pelvic balance and reducing tension that can affect how the baby positions itself in the womb. It is not a technique used to physically turn a breech baby — instead, it works to create the conditions that allow the baby to find an optimal position naturally.

Who Is It For?

Pregnant mothers at any stage who want to:

  • Support pelvic balance and comfort throughout pregnancy
  • Address pelvic, sacral, or low-back tension
  • Prepare the body for labor and delivery
  • Support optimal fetal positioning, particularly with a breech or transverse baby later in pregnancy

How Is It Performed?

The Webster Technique involves a gentle, low-force adjustment to the sacrum, combined with soft tissue work on the round ligaments that support the uterus. There is no twisting, no popping, and no abdominal pressure. Most Webster Technique visits take less than 30 minutes.

When to Start

Many mothers begin Webster Technique care in the second trimester, though it can be appropriate earlier or later. For mothers whose babies are in a breech position, care often begins around 30 to 34 weeks. The right timing depends on your individual pregnancy — we will discuss what makes sense for you at your first visit.

Common Reasons Mothers Visit

How Chiropractic Care Can Support You Through Pregnancy

Mothers come to us throughout their pregnancy for many different reasons. Here are some of the most common.

Low Back Pain & Lumbar Discomfort

As the belly grows, your center of gravity shifts forward and your low back works harder to keep you upright. Many mothers experience low back tightness or pain in the second and third trimesters. Gentle adjustments aim to ease this tension and support a more comfortable posture.

Pelvic Pain & SPD

Symphysis pubis dysfunction (SPD), pelvic girdle pain, and general pelvic discomfort are common during pregnancy. Webster Technique and gentle pelvic adjustments aim to support better balance across the pelvic joints.

Sciatica During Pregnancy

The growing uterus and shifts in posture can put pressure on the sciatic nerve, creating sharp pain that runs through the buttocks and down the leg. Chiropractic care aims to reduce that pressure and ease nerve irritation.

Hip Pain & Round Ligament Pain

Pregnancy hormones soften the ligaments around the hips and pelvis, which can lead to hip discomfort and sharp ligament pain. Gentle soft tissue work and adjustments aim to support these areas as your body changes.

Headaches & Neck Tension

Postural shifts during pregnancy commonly produce tension in the upper back, shoulders, and neck. Gentle upper-body adjustments aim to ease that tension.

Sleep & Comfort

When the pelvis is balanced and muscle tension is reduced, many mothers report sleeping more comfortably during pregnancy.

Preparation for Labor & Delivery

Pelvic balance and mobility may support a smoother labor experience. Many mothers choose Webster Technique care in the third trimester as part of their birth preparation.

Chiropractic care is not a substitute for prenatal medical care. Please continue to work with your OB/GYN or midwife throughout your pregnancy.
Care That Adapts

Care for Every Trimester of Your Pregnancy

Your body changes at every stage of pregnancy, and so does our care.

Weeks 1–13
First Trimester

Early pregnancy care is gentle and supportive. Many mothers come in for fatigue, early back discomfort, or simply to feel their best as their body begins to change. Adjustments during this stage are particularly low-force.

Weeks 14–27
Second Trimester

As the belly grows and posture shifts, many mothers begin to notice low-back, hip, and pelvic discomfort. This is often the most active phase of prenatal chiropractic care.

Weeks 28–40+
Third Trimester

This is when Webster Technique work is most commonly emphasized. Care focuses on pelvic balance, comfort during the final weeks, and supporting the body's preparation for labor.

After Delivery
Postpartum

Many mothers continue with care to support recovery, postural changes from breastfeeding and carrying a newborn, and the demands of new motherhood. Our pediatric chiropractors also welcome your baby for their first gentle checkup.

Common Questions

Pregnancy Chiropractic Care: Common Questions

Is it safe to see a chiropractor while pregnant?
Yes. Prenatal chiropractic care is widely recognized as safe when performed by a chiropractor trained in pregnancy-specific techniques. We use gentle, low-force adjustments with pregnancy-appropriate positioning at every visit.
When during pregnancy can I start chiropractic care?
You can start at any stage of pregnancy. Many mothers begin in the second trimester when posture starts to shift, but care can be appropriate from the first trimester through delivery and beyond.
Can I see a chiropractor in my first trimester?
Yes. First-trimester care is particularly gentle and is well-suited to mothers who want to support their body as it begins to change.
What is the Webster Technique?
The Webster Technique is a specific chiropractic analysis and adjustment used during pregnancy. It focuses on the sacrum and surrounding pelvic ligaments to support pelvic balance and create conditions that may help the baby find an optimal position.
Can a chiropractor help support a breech baby?
The Webster Technique is often chosen by mothers whose babies are in a breech position. It does not physically turn the baby — it works to support pelvic balance and reduce tension that can affect how the baby positions itself. We recommend starting Webster work around 30 to 34 weeks for breech presentation, in coordination with your OB/GYN or midwife.
Can chiropractic help with pregnancy back pain and sciatica?
Many mothers come to us specifically for low-back pain, sciatica, hip pain, or pelvic discomfort. Gentle adjustments aim to support better alignment and ease nerve and muscle tension.
Will the adjustment hurt me or the baby?
No. The adjustments we use are low-force, with no twisting and no popping. There is no pressure on the abdomen at any stage of pregnancy. Most mothers find the visits very comfortable.
How often should I come in during pregnancy?
That depends on your stage of pregnancy and what you are hoping to address. We discuss a recommended schedule with you after your first visit, and you are always in control of the care plan.
Does insurance cover prenatal chiropractic care?
Coverage varies by carrier and plan. Call us at (816) 895-1800 and we will help you confirm your coverage before your first visit.
What should I wear to my prenatal chiropractic appointment?
Comfortable, loose-fitting clothing that allows easy movement. You will not need to change into a gown.
How soon can I see a chiropractor after giving birth?
Many mothers come in within the first few weeks after delivery to support recovery. We tailor postpartum care to your individual delivery experience and how you are feeling.
